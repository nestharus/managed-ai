# From an accurate caption to a continuing inquiry

This is a connected, authored teaching construction. The exhibition, objects and brief are fictional. The invitation and receiving note are completed written artifacts; visitor reception and learning effects have not been observed.

## The initial material and duty

A small exhibition has three accurate supplied captions:

- **Cup:** its handle has a visible repair. The record does not identify who repaired it or when.
- **Ticket:** a date is printed on it. The record does not establish who used it or whether it was used.
- **Notebook:** it contains a table of measurements. The purpose of the measurements is not established by the supplied record.

The source material establishes no common owner, event or explanation linking the objects. The brief asks for an optional invitation that helps visitors develop relationships among them. Keep the captions accurate, leave the originals unmoved and unaltered, and allow participation without writing. Reproductions may be arranged.

The captions already fulfill their factual duty. The stronger contribution can develop from what is adequate now.

## Project a stronger contribution

The hypothesis is that an invitation could help a visitor construct an inquiry. Follow it forward: a connection could open a question, which could call for a source or comparison. Its support might differ depending on whether the connection is historical, formal, fictional or personal.

Follow it again. An invitation requiring one intended answer could close inquiry. An invitation to invent any story could obscure the difference between conjecture and established history. Required writing would contradict the brief.

These prospective consequences make particular relationships worth developing. They guide the artifact without supplying an observed visitor outcome or a complete museum-design taxonomy.

## Let the partial artifact participate

A first draft says:

> Choose two objects. Tell the story that connects them.

“The story” can imply an established connection and asks for a completed narrative. The model can inspect that wording against the source and brief, then make a justified editorial change. A visitor encounter would answer the further question of how people actually receive it.

The draft exposes a useful distinction between asking for a finished answer and opening a relationship for development. Construct that difference in the activity itself.

## Construct the invitation

The revised card is:

> **Make a connection**
>
> Choose two objects. What relationship is worth exploring?
>
> Point to something in the objects or their captions that helps you begin.
>
> What would help you develop that relationship further?
>
> You may point, speak, sketch, or simply keep looking.

A nearby support line reads:

> The captions state what the supplied records establish. A proposed connection can become a question, an interpretation or a story; it is not automatically a shared history of the objects.

The contribution now has a written body another activity can encounter. It offers several forms of participation without asking visitors to adopt the designer's technical vocabulary.

## A shared source does not create a shared verdict

The archivist's interest is that interpretation not silently become attribution. The educator wants a visitor to do something beyond recite captions. A visitor can pursue another interest.

The card offers a contact among those interests: source support, a proposed relation and a contribution that could develop it. It does not settle every interpretation. A specific claim that the ticket proves who repaired the cup still needs evidence. The original captions can remain unchanged while their arrangement and receiving use develop.

## Construct an observer only where it serves the work

The proposed staff receiving note is:

> Receive the visitor's actual contribution before deciding what it means. Ask what in the objects or captions matters to their connection. If historical attribution is consequential, distinguish the supplied record from the proposed link. Develop a relevant next source, comparison or arrangement where available. A person may decline, disagree, or offer a better question; none of those responses alone establishes failure or success.

This gives the proposed encounter a body without inventing its return. An unexpected question could make a new source worth obtaining. A visitor's disinterest could concern the occasion, wording or arrangement; that observation would need interpretation. These remain hypothetical branches.

## Carry forward a partial result

The card and note can support a draft review or authorized deployment decision. Their useful residue includes the actual wording, source limits and contrast that changed the first version.

“Ask open-ended questions” would be too loose for some later uses; requiring every future exhibit to copy this card would be too narrow. Another task can reconstruct the relationship through different material or already possess an adequate invitation. Retain what lets its next participant continue.

## Return to the wider field

The nearest child is another wording revision. Compare it with an actual authorized receiving situation, a useful arrangement of reproductions or a staff practice for developing unexpected questions. Work forward into what each could enable and backward into the participation it needs.

A new return might reveal a tiny wording issue as the bottleneck or make further wording work unnecessary. The draft can also fulfill its commission and remain complete. Investigate visitor use when a consequential decision would consume that evidence; keep the unobserved efficacy claim scoped until then.

## What the example teaches

The hypothesis generated work without dictating a winning ending. A partial artifact exposed a distinction, a producing change made it usable, and a receiving note opened a further contact. Different interests shared source material while preserving their differences. The resulting artifacts changed the available continuations.

That connected written construction fulfills this chapter's teaching duty. Its practical effect on visitors remains open. [Foundations](foundations.md) develops the relationships; [other worked examples](worked-examples.md) identify recorded executions and primary mechanisms.
