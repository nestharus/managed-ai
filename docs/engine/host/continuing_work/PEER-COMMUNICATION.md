# Communicate around a selected first-view boundary

Use this operation only when the present comparison needs each relevant
participant's own initial view before the other's interpretation. Ordinary
integrated construction and direct peer collaboration do not require such a
boundary.

Name the useful first body before treating later agreement as convergence. A
coarse forecast already preserved in a preparation or actual return can be
adequate; an extended document, a special schema and a duplicate capture are
not automatically necessary. State what that body lets the comparison
distinguish. Different agents or separate filenames alone do not establish
separate initial interpretation.

Keep the sender's readiness separate from the recipient's readiness. Until the
relevant recipient has confirmed its selected first body, send readiness and
the exact artifact identity, or a legitimate missing-source inquiry, without a
synopsis, favored conclusion, diagnosis or other answer-bearing preview. An
intention to prepare is not a completed-boundary confirmation. A sender's own
completed capture does not establish the recipient's state.

After the relevant recipient has acknowledged the needed first body, exchange
the substantive material directly and develop the shared consequence. No
central semantic merger or all-participant barrier is required: open the
exchange for the pair or actual overlap that is ready. Revise the grouping and
responsibilities when the material makes that useful.

If a semantic preview arrives early, preserve what existed before it and the
actual newly supplied meaning. An earlier adequate coarse prediction remains
an earlier adequate coarse prediction. A later elaboration is informed by the
new exposure and must be described that way. Do not inflate the event into
loss of all original prediction, rewrite the past as unexposed, or
automatically discard useful joint construction. Repeat a fresh bounded
occurrence only where the lost comparison matters; supply original inputs and
the changed participation instruction without the old answer as a target.

The rule governs a declared evidence boundary, not a claim of private
cognitive isolation. Actual retained bodies, message order and participant
accounts support a qualified sequence. Custody tools preserve those
occurrences; they do not determine whether the selected first body was
sufficient or the later interpretation was genuinely changed.
